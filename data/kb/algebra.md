# Algebra Fundamentals

## Quadratic Equations
The standard form of a quadratic equation is: $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $x$ represents an unknown, and $a$, $b$, and $c$ represent known numbers, with $a \neq 0$.
The solutions are given by the quadratic formula:
$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$

The term $b^2 - 4ac$ is the discriminant ($\Delta$).
- If $\Delta > 0$, there are two distinct real roots.
- If $\Delta = 0$, there is exactly one real root (a double root).
- If $\Delta < 0$, there are two complex roots.

## Common Mistakes in Algebra
- Forgetting to distribute a negative sign: $-(x - 5)$ is $-x + 5$, not $-x - 5$.
- Incorrectly expanding $(a+b)^2$: It is $a^2 + 2ab + b^2$, NOT $a^2 + b^2$.
- Dividing by zero: When solving algebraic equations, you must check that any expression you divide by is not zero.

## Sequence and Series
- Arithmetic Progression (AP): $n$-th term is $a_n = a + (n-1)d$. Sum of $n$ terms is $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d]$.
- Geometric Progression (GP): $n$-th term is $a_n = a r^{n-1}$. Sum of $n$ terms is $S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$ for $r \neq 1$. Infinite sum ($|r| < 1$) is $S = \frac{a}{1 - r}$.
